About this map

Not a navigation tool. It does not know where you are, and a map on a screen is no substitute for a paper map and a compass on the day.

What the picture is

The National Map's topographic base map from the U.S. Geological Survey, as the site serves it: contours, water, roads, trails, boundaries and names, at fourteen scales from the whole country to a few city blocks. Imagery shows aerial photographs with the same lines and names over them. Beyond the United States and its territories the site has only its whole-world picture, so there this app shows that picture enlarged and says so.

The ticks on the map's four edges, with the degrees and minutes at the left and at the foot, and the small crosses inside, are this app's own: the collar of a USGS quadrangle, drawn for the view on screen. They stand at every whole step of latitude and longitude, the smallest of 1″, 2″, 5″, 10″, 15″, 20″, 30″, 1′, 2′, 2.5′, 5′, 7.5′, 15′, 30′, 1°, 2°, 5°, 10° and 20° that keeps them at least 120 px apart, and the caption line names the step. The 7.5′ and 2.5′ steps are the quadrangle's own: a 1:24 000 quadrangle covers 7.5′ of latitude and of longitude, and marks a cross every 2.5′.

The map is in Web Mercator, the projection its pictures are cut in, so its scale grows toward the poles. The scale bar is true along the latitude it names, the middle of the view; the ticks and crosses are true positions everywhere. A tapped point's coordinates are read from the same projection, to four decimal places, about 11 m.

The map keeps the site's colors in both of the phone's themes, and the collar keeps its one style over it; only the chrome around the map follows the theme. That is a display choice.

How the data gets here

This is an online app. Snuggery lets it show pictures from the one site it lists, basemap.nationalmap.gov, once you allow it, and nothing else: no other site, no scripts or styles from the web, and nothing sent. Each piece of the map is a picture 256 px square, asked for while you look, at most six at a time, and kept while the app is open. The site sees each request, with your IP address, and each address says which area and which scale is on screen.

If the site is not allowed, or the phone is offline, the map says so in one sentence; the collar, the scale and a tapped point's coordinates still work, because this app works them out itself. To see the map, allow the site in Snuggery: open the app's menu and choose Sites This App Uses.

Drag to move the map, pinch to zoom toward your fingers, and double-tap to zoom in on a place. Tap a place for its coordinates. With a keyboard, the arrow keys move the map, plus and minus zoom, and Enter marks the middle. The units key cycles the scale between kilometers and miles. The last key in the column, or the F key, hides the controls; the corner key that stays, or Escape, brings them back.
