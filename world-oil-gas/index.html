What the picture is

Each country is colored by its production in the year on the player, in the unit shown, on a continuous log scale between the legend’s printed ends. A country left plain has no figure in the file for that year, which is not the same as zero, or produced nothing; its card says which. The map hatches nothing.

The strip under the map, the Ledger, is the world’s output for the year shown laid out as shares: one block per producing country, widest first, each as wide as its figure over the world’s (the file’s World series, energy-equivalent, as the Energy Institute publishes it), and every producer under 1 % together in the hatched end. Tap a block for that country’s card. In Cumulative each block is the country’s total to the year shown. A former state (the USSR, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia) gives way to its members in any year its largest member (Russia, Czechia, Serbia) has a figure of its own, so no barrel is counted twice; in Cumulative the former state’s block is what it produced until then, and its label says to which year. The Ledger says nothing about reserves, consumption, or who owns the fields.

Each field is a disc in its fuel’s color. The tracker gives one reported rate per unit, for its newest data year, and no series, so while the fields follow the year a disc’s size is this app’s estimate: the reported rate scaled by its country’s output that year over its output in the rate’s year (at most three times; the world’s when the country is not matched; unscaled when that year has no figure). A unit appears as a ring in its discovery year and fills from its production start, or from its discovery when no start is given. Units with neither date appear, filled, the year before their data year, where the estimate starts. A pale disc has no rate reported. Every disc has a thin rim, which keeps a gas disc apart from a country of its own lightness. The tracker’s outlines are drawn when zoomed in; a ring that does not fall near its own unit is left out.

Cumulative adds up every year from the first with data to the year shown: a year with no data adds nothing, and a series that has ended keeps its total. Play runs eight years a second.

How the data gets here

A yearly build writes data/snapshot.json from Our World in Data’s energy dataset; data/world.json and data/relief.jpg come from Natural Earth, and data/fields.json from a tracker release dropped in by hand. A scheduled refresh replaces data/snapshot.json. The map re-reads the three files every time it is opened and whenever new data lands while it is open; the app itself never goes online.
