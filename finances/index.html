About Finances

What the Balance is

The T at the top of Overview is the household’s balance sheet as an accountant draws it: what is owned on the left, what is owed on the right. Each block of ink is one account, fund, vehicle, home or loan, as deep as its value at the scale the ruler prints, one pixel for a fixed amount; the most liquid hang first. Both sides close on a double rule. The empty space under the debts, down to that rule, is what would be left if everything owed were paid from everything owned: the net worth. When more is owed than owned, the space is under what is owned instead, and says so.

It shows the values in this file and nothing else. It leaves out what no file lists, such as furniture or a debt to a friend; a pension or unvested shares when the snapshot does not count them; and tax that selling anything would cost. The home, the vehicles and the loans are estimates by the methods Owned names, not prices anyone has paid. A block under one pixel deep is drawn one pixel deep, so a small account still shows; its label and its card give its value.

How the numbers are made

Balances and transactions come from the banks over PSD2 account information, read once a day. Fund units are counted from the transfers that bought them and priced at the fund’s published net asset value (NAV); they drift from the provider’s own count until they are set from it again, and Savings says how long ago that was.

No one reports the home, the vehicles or the loans. Each starts from a value set by hand in assets.json on a date, then moves: the home by Statistics Norway’s price index for existing dwellings, a vehicle by a depreciation curve for its age, a loan as an annuity paid down month by month at its stated rate. Each says under its value which method it used.

Spending counts payments out over the window, leaving out moves between the household’s own accounts, money put into its own savings and income. Cash flow counts every payment in and out by calendar month, leaving out moves between its own accounts, and stops at the last whole month.

Sources and credits

Home index: Statistics Norway (SSB), table 07221, the price index for existing dwellings, under NLOD, Norway’s license for open government data (data.norge.no/nlod/en). The cut followed is printed under the home’s value. SSB does not endorse this app.

Accounts and transactions: the household’s own banks, through Enable Banking, a licensed PSD2 account-information provider. Exchange rates, where a holding is quoted in another currency: Norges Bank’s daily reference rates, credited beside the rate. Fund prices: as holdings.json sets them; NOTES.md says on what terms.

Type: Ysabeau Office by Christian Thalmann (Catharsis Fonts), SIL Open Font License 1.1; a subset is in fonts/ with its license.

How the data gets here

A scheduled job in the household’s own copy of the repository reads the banks once a day, values the rest and writes data/snapshot.json; a Shortcut carries it to this phone, and the app reads it again whenever it comes back to the screen. The app itself reaches no network.
