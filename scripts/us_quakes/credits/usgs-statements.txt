Magnitude Types
https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/magnitude-types

"Mww (Moment W-phase)(generic notation Mw) — Derived from a centroid moment tensor inversion of the
W-phase"

"Mwc (centroid) — Derived from a centroid moment tensor inversion of the long-period surface waves"

"Mwb (body wave) — Derived from moment tensor inversion of long-period (~20-200 s; pass band based
on size of EQ) body-waves (P- and SH)."

"Mwr (regional) — Based on the scalar seismic-moment of the earthquake, derived from moment tensor
inversion of the whole seismogram at regional distances"

"Ms20 or Ms (20sec surface wave) — A magnitude based on the amplitude of Rayleigh surface waves
measured at a period near 20 sec."

"mb (short-period body wave) — Based on the amplitude of 1st arriving P-waves at periods of about 1
s."

"Mfa (felt-area magnitude) — An estimate of body-wave (mb) magnitude based on the size of the area
over which the earthquake was felt"

"ML Ml, or ml (local) — The original magnitude relationship defined by Richter and Gutenberg in 1935
for local earthquakes."

"mb_Lg, mb_lg, or MLg (short-period surface wave) — A magnitude for regional earthquakes based on
the amplitude of the Lg surface waves as recorded on short-period instruments."

"Md or md (duration) — Based on the duration of shaking as measured by the time decay of the
amplitude of the seismogram."

"Mi or Mwp (integrated p-wave) — Based on an estimate of moment calculated from the integral of the
displacement of the P wave recorded on broadband instruments."

"Mh — Non-standard magnitude method. Generally used when standard methods will not work."

"Mint (intensity magnitude) — A magnitude estimated from the maximum reported intensity, typically
for earthquakes occurring before seismic instruments were in general use."

Alert Level System
https://www.usgs.gov/programs/VHP/alert-level-system

"NORMAL: Volcano is in typical background, noneruptive state or, after a change from a higher level,
volcanic activity has ceased and volcano has returned to noneruptive background state."

"ADVISORY: Volcano is exhibiting signs of elevated unrest above known background level or, after a
change from a higher level, volcanic activity has decreased significantly but continues to be
closely monitored for possible renewed increase."

"WATCH: Volcano is exhibiting heightened or escalating unrest with increased potential of eruption,
timeframe uncertain, OR eruption is underway but poses limited hazards."

"WARNING: Hazardous eruption is imminent, underway, or suspected."

"GREEN: Volcano is in typical background, non-eruptive state or, after a change from a higher level,
volcanic activity has ceased and volcano has returned to noneruptive background state."

"YELLOW: Volcano is exhibiting signs of elevated unrest above known background level or, after a
change from a higher level, volcanic activity has decreased significantly but continues to be
closely monitored for possible renewed increase."

"ORANGE: Volcano is exhibiting heightened or escalating unrest with increased potential of eruption,
timeframe uncertain, OR eruption is underway with no or minor volcanic-ash emissions [ash- plume
height specified, if possible]."

"RED: Eruption is imminent with significant emission of volcanic ash into the atmosphere likely OR
eruption is underway or suspected with significant emission of volcanic ash into the atmosphere
[ash-plume height specified, if possible]."
