About this data

What the streaks are

Each streak is a tracer carried by the forecast’s wind: it moves the way the wind at that place blows, at its speed, for the hour on the slider. The caption under the map states the exposure. At “1 s = 24 h”, one second of motion is 24 hours of that wind, held still at the hour shown, while play moves the hour on at 5 hours a second.

On the flat map the same wind runs faster toward the poles, because the map stretches there; on the globe it slows toward the edge, where the sphere turns away. So a streak’s length and speed on screen are not a measure of the wind’s strength. The colors and a tap are.

Neither a streak’s direction nor its speed is invented. Where streaks gather is partly the air and partly the picture: they bunch where the air converges, but also where the picture shrinks the ground under them (toward the equator on the flat map, toward the edge on the globe), and otherwise their spacing is random.

The forecast is the model’s value at one point every 2 degrees, about 220 km apart, sampled from a much finer grid, so the flow runs smoothly across coasts, straits and mountains smaller than this sampled grid can show.

How the data gets here

The map reads data/snapshot.json every time it is opened, and again whenever new data lands while it is open. A scheduled job rewrites that file and a Shortcut carries it in; the app itself never goes online.
